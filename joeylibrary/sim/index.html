Bookcases and shelves

Every bookcase has a letter, A to D from left to right as you face them from the room, and every shelf a number counted from the top: B3 is the third shelf down in bookcase B, and B top is on top of it. Below you can have the bookcases numbered instead (1, 2, 3, from 0, or as 01, 02) and the shelves counted from 0, with two digits or as a, b, c. Give either a name if you like, and give a bookcase a number of its own with Number. Bookcases can also be gathered into named zones under Zones; the zone's letter or number then comes first (A-B3). The other furniture books can go on is listed below the bookcases: name its shelves the same way, and give it a number to have its shelves written like a bookcase's instead of K1, Chatol 2 and so on. A book's place is kept on the shelf itself, so it stays with that shelf when the shelf is moved or the numbers change.

A BILLY has a bottom, a top and one fixed shelf in the middle that is screwed in. The four other shelves rest on pins in rows of holes 32 mm apart, one group below the fixed shelf and one above. Click a green shelf to pick it, then click a hole (or use ▲ ▼) to move it; click a hole with nothing picked to add a shelf there. Two shelves need at least one free hole between them. Books on a shelf that moves go with it, books that no longer fit are laid down, and books on a shelf you take out fall.

Other furniture books can go on

IKEA does not publish the heights of the holes. These are read off the assembly drawing and the 32 mm hole spacing, so measure a real BILLY to correct them: the height of the fixed shelf and of the lowest and highest hole in each group. The 35 cm height extension is drawn without holes. Zones

Gather bookcases, and any other furniture books can go on, into zones the way a library does: one for fantasy, one for science fiction. Each zone gets a letter or a number, written in front of the bookcase and the shelf, so the third shelf in the second bookcase of zone A is A-B3, or A-02-3, 1-2-3 and so on with other formats (bookcases and shelves are chosen under Bookcases). The bookcases in a zone count from the first one in it, in the order shown here; use ▲ ▼ to change it. A piece is in one zone at most. Zones only change how places are written: the books stay where they are.

Book types and their real sizes

Each type has a page size (the trim) and a paper thickness. A hardcover's boards stand about 3 mm proud of the pages at the top, bottom and fore-edge, and each board is about 2.8 mm thick. Thickness comes from the page count: two pages make one leaf, and a leaf is 0.095 mm in a mass-market paperback and 0.11–0.115 mm in trade paperbacks and hardcovers. Those figures come from the measured thicknesses of real books in the top-100 list.

"Trade edition" means the trade paperback: bigger page, better paper. "Travel edition" usually means the British airport/export edition, a C-format trade paperback sold at airports before the small B-format paperback comes out, or the small pocket/mass-market size made for travelling. Denmark has no fixed pocket size, but the printers' A–D format letters are used.
